## Phase 3 — Live site (Milestone A)

- **M3.4 Environment and stressors.** The watched site gets daily weather (`engine/site/weather.ts`): temperature from the seasonal curve plus a slow swing, rain, storms and snow, daylight by latitude, and the kind of year rolled each Thawmonth, a drought or hard winter following the world's climate spells still running when history ended (`world.climateEpochs`, which also names them as causes). Fields grow a crop from sowing (Seed and Bloom) to harvest (Goldmonth and Harvestmonth) with soil that wears and rests, frost that hurts and ruins, and drought that withers fields far from water (`crops.ts`); a poor harvest names its dry spell and frosts. The steward cuts meals to ¾ or ⅔ when the stores will not reach the next harvest (`site_meals_cut`, a mood entry and a capped Food need), keeps seed back for sowing, and keeps firewood for a winter's burn; food spoils by the month; a well with no river or sea runs low after 30 dry days. Lit hearths on cold nights and lightning start fires that spread by fuel, wind and snow and are fought with buckets (`fire.ts`, auto-pause); burnt homes are rebuilt and their households lodge with kin. Game, berries and fish thin and recover, animals wander, and felled woods regrow in 8–15 years (`wild.ts`). Nineteen event types are content (`content/weather.ts`). On screen: crop stages, snow, fire and animals on the map and in the Key, a weather chip beside the season, a Season section on the Site tab (the year, harvest, stores against the harvest, firewood, wild land, spoilage, the steward's meals), why lines and lodging on the person page, and field, woods and burnt-home tile lines. Wares now take a quarter log. History and the drama baseline are unchanged.
- **Process: lean mock-up round and milestone pipeline** (docs only). Thread times from M2.1 to M3.4 measured and saved under `/mnt/project-files/process/thread-times.md`; AGENTS.md and AGENT_BRIEF describe the lean round, the pipeline, merge-on-approval and the 15-minute tuning box; AGENT_BRIEF's code map now covers the site engine, playback and the scripts; PHASE_3 milestones M3.5–M3.12 carry reading lists.
- **History playback on the world map.** After a world is generated (the Generate button, or a link with no year, open page or watched site), its 250 years play back year by year on the map, from the approved calm mock-up (v4). Speeds ½ to 60 years a second (default 4; at 15 only big effects at 40% length, at 30 and 60 a time-lapse). Borders cross-fade between years, towns grow, capitals get a double ring, and the map is labelled only with nation names. Only story events animate, each in place with a few words beside it and at most 3 at once: new realms, wars and battles (war lines along the shared border), peace, razings (soot that fades over 12 years), conquests, famine and plague of importance 7 or more, disputed thrones, and rare moments (legends, legendary items made or stolen, great beasts slain) with a gold banner and a longer slow-down. Banners carry a line in story voice; the camera can follow big events; trade threads (each decade's trades, cross-border ones in gold) are off by default. The year slider's play button opens the playback from its year; "Play history after generating" turns auto-play off. Engine: `world.playback` records display borders in the years they move (computed apart from the rules' territory, so history and the baseline are unchanged), sites and capitals every year, and each decade's trades (S1: 51 border years, 292 KB, about 3 s generation). Effects, tags and story lines are content (`content/playback.ts`). Trade between nations is rare (19 of 828 trades on S1 cross a border, all after year 200); long-distance trade belongs to M4.4.
- **Phase 2 playtest fixes.** Famine now shrinks towns: migrants shun a site that starved in the last 5 years (`famineShunYears`), where before a drought-struck town with spare room drew migrants every year and ended at its peak (S3 Kadina, 352 to 1,009 through six famines). A succession after a dispute says who won ("after winning the quarrel with …" or "having pushed aside …, the heir by custom"), and razing deaths name the place ("the burning of the village"). The drama tool adds `faminePeak` (sites with 3+ famines in the last 25 years still at their peak: 4 to 1 on 10 seeds); the baseline and smoke digests are regenerated. Three site edge cases the new history exposed are fixed: no stand-in adult when elders are coming, a job failing two requirements no longer reads as able, and someone living alone eats at a neighbour's table when there is no hall or nobody else lives alone. Humans at 73% on S5 stay as the race rule allows.
- **M3.3 Work, skills and steward AI.** Thirteen jobs as content (`content/jobs.ts`: farm, forage, fish, hunt, fell, quarry, mine, cook, build, craft tools, craft wares, heal, haul) gated by attributes (R-25), with ten new mundane skills in `content/abilities.ts`. Skill grows by doing toward a ceiling set by potential (R-27), half again faster beside someone 3 or more points better, and `site_skilled` marks competent, skilled and master. Output = attributes × skill × tools × mood × age band. The stockpile holds items (`content/items.ts`: grain, fish, game, berries and cooked meals counted in meals; logs, stone, ore, salt, tools, wares, herbs), each mapping to one world good so leaving the site sums them back exactly. Cooks stretch 3 raw meals into 4 and a cooked meal is remembered; tools raise output by a quarter and wear out. Artifact and book entries give the world's items effects (attribute bonus, combat value, magic power held to one tier above the holder's and never past Legendary) and downsides: envy among the greedy, thieves who steal the item in a theft breakdown (`site_item_stolen`), and curses that trouble the holder. Notables bring their items to the site. `content/buildings.ts` adds the workshop and the wall. A daily steward (`engine/site/work.ts`) raises homes for homeless or crowded households and a workshop (`site_build_begun`, `site_built`; haulers carry materials to the site), repairs worn buildings, keeps food workers between 50% and 85% of hands by days of stores (`site_food_short`), keeps tools and materials stocked, and assigns people by suitability, current job and liked traits. A town with no room for a workshop crafts in the stockpile yard. The site view shows each person's work, skills with their ceiling and carried items, the Site tab shows stock by good and a Work section, and buildings under construction draw faded with their progress. About 450–590 days per second for 150 residents in Node; one speed guard of 2.5 s a year replaces the two 1 s guards.
- **M3.2 Daily life core.** The site runs in four blocks a day (night, morning, afternoon, evening): people sleep at home, work the fields, play, eat with their household, socialise on the green and worship at the shrine on rest days, walking there by grid A* (`engine/site/path.ts`: 4-neighbour, paths and floors cheap, water and walls blocked except bridges and doors, a per-block expansion budget with straight-line fallback, a route cache). Eight needs (`content/life.ts`: food, water, rest, warmth from the cell's seasonal temperature, safety, company, purpose, and faith where the culture weighs it at 3+), mood from low needs, fading memories and traits, stress below mood −30, and breakdowns by trait (tantrum, despair, wandering off, theft) that witnesses remember; every resident gets one or two traits at promotion (notables keep theirs). Children under 3 go where a household adult goes ("With Ragfa"), and people who live alone eat at the hall or on the green. Meals come from the stockpile; until M3.3 brings jobs, field work brings in the site's normal-year history harvest. New events: `site_tantrum`, `site_despair`, `site_wandered`, `site_theft`, `site_returned`, `site_stores_empty` (breakdowns auto-pause). The inspector shows activity, needs with "why" lines ("Idle: no work for 3 days"), mood parts, stress against its threshold and traits; people walk between blocks at 1× and 4×; fields are labelled; the time bar names the block just run and rest days, and stays pinned on phones; the Site pane shows days of food and the temperature. The worker sends a snapshot of people at most every 100 ms, so the main thread's copy and the map now update while playing, and Back after "Open in Legends" returns to the site. About 700–800 days per second for 150 residents in Node.
- **M3.1 Embark, local map and promotion.** "Watch this site" on any standing site (Legends site page and map inspector) and "Found an outpost" on empty land with fertility 40 or more that a people can reach (8–14 settlers leave the nearest site of that people, which loses them, and an `outpost_founded` event is recorded). `engine/site/` builds a 96×96 local map from the site cell and half of each neighbour (elevation, river, sea and shore, marsh, trees by biome, stone, ore and clay from deposits) and a starting layout sized to the population: homes, fields, well, stockpile, shrine, a hall at a capital and a dock on the coast. Promotion keeps notables' records and creates the other residents with `rollAptitude`, matching the site's race, culture and age bands, in households by the culture's marriage custom (no household without an adult; shared names get a patronym). Sites over 150 people follow about 150 (the ruler's household, every living notable, then the homes nearest the hall) and keep the rest as counts; goods become stockpile counts by the followed share, and leaving puts everything back exactly (`WorldState.activeSite`, rules in `content/site.ts`). The site runs daily in a worker at pause, 1×, 4× or 16× with the measured rate shown. The site view matches the approved mock-up: coloured letters on tinted tiles with elevation shading, role-coloured rings for people, an overview map, a Key, Inspect / Site / People / Chronicle tabs (households, filters, the legends lines with "why?"), an auto-pause banner with category chips, keyboard control, URL state (`site`, `day`, `open`, `z`, `at`) and a phone layout. `npm run site -- --seed S1 --site 6` prints the promotion and writes a map PNG; the Chromium smoke watches a site and checks the local-map hash against `tests/fixtures/smoke-localmap-digest.json`.

## Phase 2 — Peoples and Legends

- **Phase 2 close.** The legacy single-settlement app is deleted (`src/sim`, `src/controller`, `src/adapters`, `src/ui`, 17 unit tests and 13 fixtures), so `/` serves the world map and legends browser; old `/?next` links still work and links the app writes drop the flag. The few page-wide styles the app borrowed from the legacy stylesheet moved into `world-map.css` (the page is pixel-identical on four views at desktop and phone width). Playtest fixes: chronicle lines that open on a beast's name ("the wolves of …") start with a capital, and map legend tick labels that would overlap are dropped (elevation's 0 m and 400 m). Boundaries now know only the four new layers.
- **M2.7 Legends browser and year slider.** A Legends pane beside the map (Map / Legends tabs on phones) with one search box and lists of civs, sites, races, figures, families, beasts, artifacts and events. Each page opens with the M2.6 biography summary, facts that link onward, and the chronicle highlights, with a toggle for every event. Every name in a line is a link, every line with causes has a "why?" that unfolds its cause chain one level per click, and event pages list what the event led to. A year slider under the map redraws borders (from the decade snapshot at or before the year), sites, ruins and lairs as they stood, with play and tick marks for wars, razings, foundings and legends; opening an event moves the slider to its year and later lines fade. Opening anything centres the map on it with a gold ring. Engine: `LegendIndex.refs` and `.effects`, `narrative/legends.ts` (`linkSegments`, `historyAt`, `locate`) and `cellsDigest`. The Chromium smoke follows a cause link to an event and back to the map, and checks the SMOKE cells hash against `tests/fixtures/smoke-cells-digest.json`, which a unit test checks in Node too, so float drift between them fails CI. The old chronicle list under the map is gone. Revised after review: event types carry a `title` and a `causeWord` as content (titles head event pages, lists and tick tooltips; cause lines read "because of", "after" or "in revenge for"), type groups in `content/legends.ts`, importance as stars, breadcrumbs, URL state (`year`, `open`, `cat`, `q`) with browser back and forward, windowed lists with sort and filter, search over every event, Expand all, participants and sibling events on event pages, a Lock year toggle that alone fades later lines, clickable aligned ticks with decade marks, 1×/4× play and keyboard stepping, and a phone layout whose Legends body scrolls.
- **M2.6 Notability and biographies.** Every history event type has 3–5 chronicle variants in the historian voice (48 new ones for the 24 most common types, drafted by a Haiku pass as content and reviewed); history events print their date as the history year ("Year 112"), `a`/`an` agree with the words filled in, and yearly rollups use plurals ("2 marriages"). Event types declare `links` (which data keys name a civ, site, beast, item or dynasty), `precision` and `rollup` labels, and validation rejects a variant that uses a key the first variant lacks. `engine/narrative/biography.ts` builds a legend index over the event list and a biography for any notable, site, civ, beast, item or dynasty: summary sentences from the records (`content/biographies.ts`) plus its 12 most important chronicle lines. The site inspector shows the site's biography; `npm run bios -- --seed S1` prints civs, the largest sites and the most famous people. A famine with no bad year of its own now names the site's earlier trouble as its cause, so the drama check's new `uncaused` count is 0 on 10 seeds; every other history number is unchanged.
- **M2.5 Notables and families.** Notable people at world tier (`engine/history/notables.ts`, `items.ts`, `people.ts`; tunables in `content/notables.ts`): each civ starts with a ruler, spouse, children and a dynasty; founders, beast-slaying heroes, war captains, famine raiders, crafters, temple scholars and strong-born mages become notables by their deeds. Notables marry (arranged cultures make alliance marriages that cut grievance), have children rolled from both parents with `rollAptitude` (bloodlines), age and die of age, famine, disease, beasts, raids, battle and razing, with killers recorded. Succession follows each culture's rule, can be disputed (rivals and oaths), and a ruler from outside the line founds a new dynasty. A notable killed by a foreigner leaves kin who swear vengeance, and a ruler's kin bring grievance to their civ. Exceptional mages may grow legendary, at most 3 alive; mages add R-24 strength to their civ. Artifacts and books are made, inherited by the culture's rule, lost, found and stolen. The site inspector shows the ruler and notables. Decade snapshots of borders and sites (`history/snapshots.ts`, run-length territory) for the M2.7 year slider; saves are gzipped with `CompressionStream`. 10 seeds: 333–638 notables alive at year 250, 30–81 successions, 1–8 disputed, up to 3 legends alive; history stays under 0.9 s per seed.
- **M2.4b History balance.** Climate epochs (`engine/history/climate.ts`): dry and cold spells of 5–20 years over part of the map raise drought or harsh-winter odds and cut yields, and their `dry_years_began` or `cold_years_began` event is a cause of the famines inside them. Site caps: a new site needs a village's capacity for its race, 5 cells of spacing and a 15-year cooldown per civ; mines go only where the civ lacks that mineral and temples once per civ per aura spot; civ count takes a ±2 seeded jitter, and every race founds a people before any race founds a second. Race balance by mechanism: Tylwyth capacity scales with mean aura in reach, Dvergar sites without ore or stone go hungry (and feed 3x with it), settlers leave from 65% of capacity, and race fertility is back to the sketches (Tylwyth 0.3, Dvergar 0.5). Diseases are content (`content/diseases.ts`: plague, summer fever, pox with infectivity, severity, age risk and immunity). Events get an importance score (base + site fame + scale + novelty); events under 3 roll up into a yearly `year_summary` and are dropped unless a kept event cites them, and `bad_harvest` is emitted only when stores run short. Tools: `tests/baseline/drama-10-seeds.json` with `npm run drama -- --baseline`, and `npm run sweep -- --rule <path> --values a,b,c`. Result on 10 seeds: 41–102 live sites, ruins on every seed, civs fall on 6, every race at 8% or more on 4 of S1–S5 (Tylwyth 6% on S5).

- **M2.3 Yearly history: life and land.** A new last `history` world-gen stage (`engine/history/`, rules in `content/history.ts`) runs `historyYears` (250) yearly steps in a fixed order: harvest (fertility-capacity yield with drought and harsh-winter rolls from the risk layers), demographics (births, deaths and ageing by band and race), famine, plague (outbreaks in sites of 200+, spreading one step a year along site links, with immunity), migration to roomier linked sites, expansion into new villages, and site kinds growing, shrinking or being abandoned, with peoples falling when their last site goes. Site links come from walking cost (and sea lanes between harbours). New events (`bad_harvest`, `famine`, `plague`, `migration`, `site_grew`, `site_abandoned`, `civ_fell`, and `site_founded` for new villages) carry cause links, so chains like famine, migration and a new village can be followed. The worker reports progress each decade ("History, year N…"). The Peoples legend lists the world's civs (fallen ones marked), ruined sites draw as a grey cross, and the site inspector shows founded and ruined years, peak population and its latest five events. The drama check prints a history table (sites, ruins, falls, population, famines, plagues, migrations, new sites, race shares, longest cause chain). Balance: death rates scale with lifespan, births slow only past 60% of capacity, each race farms foreign biomes at half yield, Dvergar sites by ore deposits feed 1.5x (ore traded for food), and race fertility is tuned up from the sketches (Tylwyth 0.3 to 0.6, Dvergar 0.5 to 0.75), so humans end at 62–68% on S1–S5 (none above 70% on 10 seeds).

- **M2.2 Settlement and sites.** A new `settle` world-gen stage (`engine/history/settle.ts`, rules in `content/settlement.ts`) founds 3–8 peoples (about one per 3,500 land cells), each with a capital and, half the time, a second site picked by the culture's preferred biomes and site preferences (river, coast, aura, plains, deposit, forest, heights), fertility and nearby route nodes. Sites have a kind (hamlet to city, fortress, mine, temple, port), a carrying capacity from fertility, fish and game within 3 cells with port, ford and road bonuses, a population in child, adult and elder bands by race, stores and the features that drew them. Territory is the nearest site by travel cost. Founding emits `civ_founded` and `site_founded` events with cause links. The viewer gains a Peoples layer (key 0), a Sites overlay (key S, glyph by kind, size by population, names) and a Site block in the inspector; `npm run worldmap -- --layer sites` and the drama check report peoples and sites.

## Phase 1 — World generation

- **M1.6 Travel and routes.** A travel-cost layer (biome, slope, river crossings, cheaper at fords and passes), a route graph joining harbours, fords, passes and fertile river mouths on each landmass plus sea lanes between harbours, and a likely-roads overlay (key R). The inspector gains a Travel row and a "Road" why line; the drama check prints nodes, edges and connectivity.
- **M1.5 Names and strategic features.** Ranges, rivers, forests, deserts, lakes, seas, islands and volcano clusters are detected and named from syllable tables, along with passes, fords, harbours, straits and bays. A Names overlay (key N) labels them by zoom, and the inspector lists a Places block.
- **M1.4 World map viewer and worker.** `/?next` opens on the world map. Generation runs in a Web Worker (`platform/worldgen-worker.ts`, `worldgen-host.ts`) with a stage-by-stage progress line, and the old map stays on screen, dimmed, until the new one arrives. `app/WorldMap.tsx` draws one base layer at a time from cached offscreen bitmaps, with pan, zoom (up to 8 px per cell, with a cell grid), a minimap and keyboard shortcuts. Rivers, lakes and sea and hillshade are overlays (new `water` render option). The legend has a value tick that follows the pointer, and a cell inspector gives "why" lines from the rule table in `content/map-why.ts`, each with a "show" link to its layer. Seed, size, layer, selected cell and zoom live in the URL (Generate pushes history; other changes replace it), and saves keep the generated map. The phone layout uses a layer chip row and a bottom-sheet inspector. The smoke test now covers generate → layer → inspect → save → reload → load.
- **Fix: rainfall bands and slope streaks.** Rainfall now sweeps all rows together with moisture mixing between neighbouring rows, blends the westerly and trade-wind sweeps across a wandering belt edge, and lets the rain belts wander too, so rainfall and biome borders no longer follow straight east-west lines. Plate-edge uplift is blurred, which removes the one-cell streaks that the 4-neighbour boundary distance carried down mountain slopes.
- **M1.3 Biomes, resources, aura, fertility and wildness.** `generateWorld(seed, params, content, onProgress?)` now takes the worldgen content and adds five stages (about 0.35 s in total at 256×256). Biomes come from a temperature × rainfall table in `content/biomes.ts` (mean temperature, with winter low gating jungle and savanna), plus alpine, swamp, coast, ocean and lake rules. Volcanoes sit inland of subducting oceanic plates. A faint aura layer has strong spots at peaks, river heads, old forests and hot springs (`auraSpots`, Phase 2 legendary-place candidates). Fifteen resources in `content/resources.ts` grow in seeded clusters with per-resource RNG streams and cell-share caps. Fertility (0–100) and wildness (0–100) layers feed Phase 2. New map layers (biome, fertility, wildness, aura, resources), drama columns and the `worldgen-check` project skill.
- **M1.2 Climate, water and erosion, plus worldgen tools.** `generateWorld` now runs terrain, erosion, climate and water stages (about 0.25 s at 256×256). Light stream-power erosion carves valleys; every cell gets distance to sea, a summer high and winter low (latitude 70°N to 10°S, lapse rate, wider swings inland), wind-swept rainfall with rain shadows, and drought, storm and snow risks. Priority flood gives every cell a downhill path to the sea, rivers above a flow threshold, lakes in larger depressions and floodplains. Cells at or below sea level are sea, including inland seas. New params `temperatureShift` and `rainfallScale`. Tools: `npm run drama` (world stats and stage timings), `npm run worldmap` (PNG per layer, Node `zlib`), layer palettes in `content/map-layers.ts`, a pure `renderLayer`, `checkWorldCells` and the `gridFromAscii` test helper.
- **M1.1 Terrain.** `generateWorld(seed, params)` builds a 256×256 heightmap in about 0.1 s: paired continental plates (inland ranges where they collide), oceanic plates (coastal ranges, island arcs, rifts), noise-warped plate borders, fractal coasts, ridged interior hills and an ocean margin. Sea level is set to hit `params.landFraction` (default 0.35); `params.continents` defaults to 4. Typed-array layers round-trip through saves. Added the `implementer` (Sonnet) and `test-runner` (Haiku) agents and the model-routing rules in `AGENTS.md`.

## Phase 0 — Reset and skeleton

- **M0.3 Engine review, sfc32 and saves.** The RNG is now `sfc32` (four 32-bit words per named stream, seeded with cyrb128 and checked against an independent reference vector). New `src/platform/save-store.ts` keeps named saves in IndexedDB with a SHA-256 checksum and one-transaction writes. `/?next` has Save, Load and Delete, and the Chromium smoke covers save → reload → load → continue.
- **M0.1–M0.2 Docs reset and slim CI.** Old docs, ADRs, probes, evidence, seed batteries, long-run tests, legacy browser specs and study scripts moved to `docs/archive/`. New short docs: vision, architecture, roadmap, decisions, content guide, deployment and the Phase 0 spec. CI is now one `core` job (format, types, boundaries, unit tests, build) and one Chromium smoke (`browser`). The boundary check rejects non-safe `Math` functions in `engine` and `content`.

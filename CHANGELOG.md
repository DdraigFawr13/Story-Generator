## Process

- **Faster tests** (Morgan approved all five changes, 2026-10-16). Tests that need an example event (a deed, a civil war, a wedding, a razing death) no longer pin one seed: each searches a fixed list of about eight seeds with `tests/unit/helpers/find-world.ts` and fails, naming the list, if none has it; "one world has every deed" checks were split so each kind is found on its own. The browser smoke computes its SMOKE digests in Node at test time (`tests/browser/smoke-expected.ts`), so the two saved digest files are gone. Unit tests share worker state (`isolate: false`) and the format check caches; the browser smoke runs against a production build with two workers, and CI caches the Chromium download. A latent race in the site smoke (reading the link before a tick in flight settled) was fixed by waiting for the link and the address bar to agree. Measured in a 4-core container: unit tests 52 s to 31 s, `npm run check` 77 s to 49 s, format re-check 12 s to under 1 s, browser smoke 97 s to 70 s.

## Phase 7 — Institutions (built early)

- **Religion: gods, high priests, prophets and holy wars** (build order 8, pulled forward; approved by Morgan on a card). Each people's religion is now a faith with named gods, two values it holds sacred and one it abhors (`content/faiths.ts`); two faiths clash when one abhors what the other holds sacred. Each faith keeps a high priest at its holy city, named again when one dies. Each decade, zealous realms (faith value 5 or more) may denounce a clashing neighbour's faith (a grievance), towns of another faith may take up their ruler's, and a ruler whose realm mostly keeps another faith may convert. Rare prophets (at most 3 alive) preach a holy war, split their faith into a new one that clashes with its parent, or carry it to a neighbour's town. A war between clashing faiths is proclaimed holy when the attacker's faith value is 6 or more, or a preached war or defiled temple stands among its causes; holy wars run longer before weariness, and a town taken in one may convert at once. Taking a holy city or a temple of another faith gives every realm of that faith a grievance. A town keeping another faith than its realm's breaks away twice as easily; free cities prefer co-religionists as allies, and realms of one faith go to war less often. Faiths, each realm's and town's faith, and the priests and prophets are in the world data for the Legends screens (UI redesign thread). 10 seeds at 256 against main: 145 faiths (31 schisms), 38 prophets, 109 denunciations, 79 holy wars of 330 wars (24%; wars 330 to 330), 117 temples or holy cities defiled, 123 towns and 14 rulers converted, 28 faiths forgotten as their peoples fell; battles 1,336 to 1,460, towns conquered 222 to 262, razed 356 to 380; history 4.92 s per world against main's 4.75 s on the same machine. Baseline regenerated; five fixture seeds re-pinned for history drift, the skill-cap test now uses a small town (SITE-A's biggest grew past the 5 s limit) and the terrain tests skip history they never read.

## Phase 5 — Magic depth (built early)

- **Generation feuds and exile** (M4.5, build order 19, P4 of the proposed next set). A killing between two houses (a murder, a plot, a raid, a duel, an execution, a burning town or a battle) can begin a blood feud (`feud_began`); when a bearer dies their child, grandchild or nearest kin of the house takes it up (`feud_inherited`, with the generation), the house owed blood strikes back (`feud_struck`, the target slain or escaping) and each killing turns the debt, until the houses wed, pay a blood price, let it lie or die out (`feud_ended`). A killer of their own people is driven to a foreign court (`notable_exiled`), as are some losers of a disputed seat and every grown claimant beaten in a succession war; an exile comes home pardoned once the ruler who drove them out is dead, or to press a claim that can start a civil war (`exile_returned`). An inheritance quarrel after a ruler's, founder's or artifact-holder's death can split a house into a branch (`house_split`, `Dynasty.branchOf`), often in feud with the old house. Allies whose ruling houses are in feud betray each other more readily. Rules in `content/feuds.ts`, engine in `history/feuds.ts`; the People room gets a "Feuds and exiles" filter. 10 seeds at 256: 194 feuds (13-29 a world), 223 inheritances, feuds reaching 3 generations in 9 seeds and 4 in one, 266 killings, 24 ended by a wedding, 70 exiles with 34 returns (14 to claim the seat), 53 houses split, no legend slain in a feud. History 3.70 s a world against main's 4.05 s in the same container (no slowdown measurable).
- **Great works and enchanting** (M5.3, build order 18, P3 of the proposed next set). A living legend, or the ruler of a rich capital (650 wealth or more), raises one great work in a lifetime at a live town of its realm, paid in the town's goods (`content/great-works.ts`, `history/great-works.ts`): a wall (towns and cities; half again the defence in war, half the deaths from a beast raid, breached when the town is taken), a bridge (a river or ford; tolls add wealth), a mage's tower (while it stands the realm keeps the builder's strong spells) or a warded grove (a wooded town, a life or earth mage or an elf; a tenth more harvest and 30% fewer plague deaths). A legend whose town fits none makes a great item instead (`great_item_made`). A work falls when its town is ruined (`great_work_fell`); raising one is a deed of renown. Each decade a Strong mage who still knows a strong spell of their school may enchant an item (`item_enchanted`) from a slain great beast's remains or the ore of a town with a deposit: a sword, helm, bow, cup, ring or crown by school, kept or given to the realm's ruler or champion; its holder fights beasts as if 40 soldiers stronger, and the item's deeds record the beasts it helped slay. 8 seeds at 256: 40 works (6 walls, 14 bridges, 11 towers, 9 groves), 2 fell, 58 enchanted items, 8 great items; legends alive still at most 3. History 5.96 s against main's 5.74 s per world (+4%) in the same slower container. The map glyph for standing works waits for the Tales map polish to merge.
- **Spellbooks and lineages** (M5.2, build order 9; Morgan's report 2026-10-14: Cerenael had no record of how he learned magic or which spells he could use). Every mage (gifted or stronger) is now apprenticed a little before they are grown (`content/lineages.ts`, `history/lineages.ts`): to a mage parent strong enough to teach them, else the best master of their people who can teach their school (legends first), else their people's college, a spellbook kept in their town, or a village hedge-witch, who teaches only a cantrip. An apprentice whose teachers have nothing more for them goes on to a master who has. Each people starts with an elder master holding its old canon book of every school its race takes to. Spells are abilities (`content/abilities.ts`: four per school, cantrip, gifted 4, strong 8, exceptional 13, inside the R-24 caps) learned one at a time from a teacher, a held book or a college; a mage's book holds the spells they knew; a legend takes a strong mage as apprentice; spellbooks pass to the dead master's apprentice; books burn when a town is razed (half of them) and are stolen in conquest; a people that loses its last knower or book of a strong spell loses it (`lore_lost`), and finds it again when a book comes back (`lore_regained`). A college spell (rain, thaw, ward, bounty) now needs one of its named spells. The People room has a Magic tab: how they learned and from whom, the line of teachers, spells with when and from whom, apprentices and spellbooks. 10 seeds at 256 against main: mages 227 to 214, books 67 to 91, legends 10.3 to 7.0 (great spells, a deed of renown, are rarer now that casters must know them), notables ever 4,056 to 4,217; per world (5 seeds) about 300 apprenticeships (205 to a teacher, 66 to a hedge-witch, 12 at a college, 8 from a book), 19 books carried by apprentices, 4 burned, 23 losses and 7 recoveries of lore, spells worked 4.6 to 2.6; history 3.54 s to 3.86 s per world on the same machine (limit 10 s). Baseline regenerated; fixtures re-pinned for history drift (named in each test).
- **Legends rise from deeds** (fix, reported by Morgan 2026-10-14: Cerenael became a legend of mind with nothing in his record). A figure of exceptional power now grows legendary only within 10 years of a deed of renown they led (slaying a beast, standing as champion, a vengeance raid, a great spell, coming home from a college, founding one, writing a book, or winning an expedition; `deeds.risePhrases`), and `legend_risen` is caused by that deed and names it ("By Year 63, after standing as champion of the Free City of Durzabur at Vultash, Uzgok ... was counted among the legends of mind"). `legendChance` 0.03 to 0.1 for those who qualify keeps legend numbers level. 10 seeds at 256: legends 100 to 103, those with a deed of renown in the 10 years before rising 40 to 103. Baseline regenerated; the deeds fixture (DH-152), the wedding fixture (SITE-M) and the two smoke digests re-pinned for history drift.
- **Dragons as individuals** (M5.6, build order 7; approved by Morgan on a card, choosing up to three at once). Every world now gets dragons with a given name, an age, a temper and a named lair (`content/dragons.ts`): up to three alive at once in a 256 world (one per 20,000 cells), lairs 48 cells apart, a successor 30 to 80 years after each death. Greedy dragons take a third of a town's stores as tribute unless a legend refuses; wrathful ones raid twice as often and may burn a town under 300 people (once in 30 years at most); slumbering ones sleep 20 to 50 years at a time. Tribute, plunder and the treasures of the figures a dragon kills fill its hoard; the hoard draws seekers (legends, then exceptional mages, then heroes), and the slayer carries it home. Nobody founds a town within 10 cells of a living dragon's lair. 10 worlds: 34 dragons, 123 tributes, 24 towns burned, 96 seekers, 4 dragons slain; history 3.51 s per world against main's 3.68 s on the same machine.

## Phase 6 — Kingdoms (built early)

- **The dragon at court** (build order 17, the dragon part of Phase 6 "Legends in politics"). Realms whose seat lies within 60 cells of a living dragon deal with it (`content/dragons.ts` `court`, `history/dragon-court.ts`), each step an event citing the harm or war behind it. A realm that has taken two harms from one dragon (a raid, a burning, a tribute, burned envoys) answers it: it buys peace with a third of its seat's food every 5 years (`dragon_pact`; the dragon then spares its towns), or, more often when its people prize war over wealth or it has a legend, puts a price on the dragon's head (`dragon_bounty_set`); a dragon that takes no tribute always gets a price. A ruler at war may court a dragon with gifts (`dragon_courted`): a greedy one takes them, a wrathful one burns the envoys 7 times in 10; a courted dragon spares the courter while its ruler lives and is loosed on the enemy's towns (`dragon_unleashed`, raiding four times as often while an enemy town is in reach; the struck realm holds it against the courter). A standing price is taken now and then (`dragon_bounty_taken`) by the payer's own legend, great mage or hero, else a legend of a friendly realm, never the payer's ruler; they hunt with a fifth of the payer's adults (at most 1,000) and three times the usual odds, and a slayer is paid (`dragon_bounty_claimed`, +5 fame). The lent host also shields them: legends died in 1 bounty hunt in 10 (Morgan's hunt rule). 8 seeds at 256: 26 dragons, 45 courtships (27 accepted), 8 unleashed raids, 17 pacts, 51 bounties, 49 bounty hunts with 4 claimed, 2 of 20 legend hunters killed, 5 dragons slain in all (2 on main). History 3.72 s against main's 3.77 s per world in the same container. Baseline regenerated; the intrigue, deeds and lore example searches gained seeds and the site fixtures moved to worlds that still fit them.
- **Diplomacy** (build order 13). Alliances, calls to arms, betrayal, vassals and peace by marriage (`content/diplomacy.ts`, `history/diplomacy.ts`), each an event citing what led to it. Each decade a realm or free city may take a linked neighbour as a sworn ally when neither holds a war-sized grudge, their ways are not too strange, and they have a reason: a shared enemy, one faith, a marriage between their houses, or trade (`alliance_sworn`). A new war calls the defender's allies before its first battle: an ally answers (`ally_answered`: half its strength stands behind its friend in that war, and the attacker resents it) or breaks faith (`call_refused`: the alliance ends and the betrayed realm holds a grievance), answering more often when its people prize honour. After ten quiet years allies whose gods parted, whose rulers are rivals, or where one grew weak may betray the other (`alliance_betrayed`), marching on them at once when one and a half times as strong. A realm that loses two towns to one twice its strength may bend the knee at the peace (`vassal_sworn`): it sends a twentieth of its food each year to the overlord's capital, the two never declare war on each other, and they stand together in war; once half the overlord's strength it may rise (`vassal_revolt`, a war), and it is free if the overlord falls (`vassal_freed`). A war may end at a wedding of the two rulers' kin (`peace_by_marriage`), often followed by an alliance. The sick abed now eat at home rather than walking to the hall. 10 seeds at 256 (per world): 19 alliances, 4.4 calls answered and 2.6 refused, 3.5 betrayals (doubled at Morgan's request), 1.9 vassals and 0.6 revolts, 1.8 wars ended by a wedding. History +1% per world against main measured in the same container (4.42 s against 4.38 s on 5 seeds). Baseline regenerated.
- **Court intrigue** (build order 10, Phase 6 "Legends in politics"). Plots and legends at court (`content/intrigue.ts`, `history/intrigue.ts`), each an event citing the wrong behind it. A beaten claimant may plot against the ruler who beat them, and an avenger against the foreign ruler or legend they swore on (`plot_struck`, `plot_foiled`): a killing is a death "at an assassin's hand" and a claimant who kills the ruler may take the seat; a plotter caught is put to death. A ruler may honour a legend of the realm (`legend_honoured`: honoured legends stay loyal), fear one (a foreign ruler whose people the legend harmed, or their own ruler when the legend outshines them) and plot against them; a legend wronged by a plot casts the ruler down (`seat_seized`, death cause `overthrow`), and a legend may take the seat at a succession by strength of arm, past the heir, who may then raise a civil war. A legend who is the ruler's rival, or a stranger to the realm's ways, may go over once in their life to a people at odds with theirs (`legend_betrayed`); an enemy ruler may pay a legend to stand aside from a war (`legend_bribed`: no host broken, no duel while it lasts); peoples a legend harmed twice set a bounty (`bounty_set`) and send a hunt, joint when two or more hold one (`legend_hunted`): a host larger than the legend can break kills them 7 times in 10, and one they can break still kills them 1 time in 10 (Morgan's call); a legend's death gets two more importance. 10 seeds at 256 (per world): 4.2 plots struck and 9.5 foiled, 1.5 seats seized by legends, 4.5 honours, 0.2 betrayals, 0.9 bribes, 1.1 bounties and 0.5 hunts (hosts of 60-220 against a legend who breaks 250-500, so most hunts fail). History 5.69 s to 5.74 s per world on the same container (+1%; about 3.9 s on the usual machine). Baseline regenerated; the three-legends stress test re-pinned to a lighter seed (named in the test).
- **Succession wars and dynasties** (build order 6, pulled forward; approved by Morgan on a card). A claimant beaten in a disputed succession may refuse to yield (`content/realms.ts` `CIVIL_WARS`: 0.7 × the people's war value ÷ 10, in a realm of 3 or more towns). Their seat and the towns nearest it rise as a realm of their own (`civil_war_began`), holding a third to three fifths of the realm's grown people by the claimant's renown against the new ruler's; a seat that rose before gives a numbered name ("the Second Warhost of Inisorc"). The claimant marches on the throne city first, then the stronger half marches each year; kin never burn a town, and luck swings twice as far. The war ends when the claimant takes the throne city (`throne_seized`), the claimant's last town falls (`revolt_crushed`), or after 5 years undecided (`realm_split`, with a grievance 4 for the old realm); the beaten leader is put to death or flees abroad. A claimant left with one town rules a free city and swears pacts, and far towns of a realm at war with itself may break away every year of the war. A ruling house that held the seat 50 years or 3 rulers gets an ending when another house takes it (`dynasty_ended`). Civil war battles get their own muster line in the play-by-play, and battles now carry their captains' names, so a claimant who later changed realms is named on the right side. 10 seeds at 256 against main: 49 civil wars (0 to 10 per world): 8 thrones seized, 30 revolts crushed, 11 splits; 4 split-off one-town free cities and 8 towns broken free during the wars (all break-aways 86 to 76); 167 ruling houses ended; wars between peoples 305 to 308, battles 1,135 to 1,287, towns conquered 194 to 258 (civil wars included), realms fallen to conquest 109 to 116; history 3.07 s to 3.19 s per world. Not built: the dynasty family tree in the Legends browser. The settlement test counts claimants' realms among the civs that join during history. Baseline regenerated. Mock-up: `/mnt/project-files/worldgen/succession-wars/`.

## Phase 4 — Living world

- **Expeditions and perilous places** (M4.7 history part, build order 12; approved by Morgan on the output mock-up card). The wild land now holds perilous places (`content/places.ts`, `history/places.ts`): about 30 ancient ruins, old mines and aura places per 256 map from before history, every ruined site but a hamlet, a barrow over one ruler in four (scaled by the people's faith; one artifact goes into the ground with them) and the empty lair of a great beast that died of age. Each year a civ may send an expedition (2% × its war + wealth + learning ÷ 15) into a place within 40 cells, or after a great beast that raided it in the last 20 years. The strongest willing figure leads (legend, exceptional mage, hero, captain, then a noble half the time); otherwise a band of commoners goes to the safer places, and a commoner who comes back with something becomes a hero. Odds follow R-24 (party plus the leader's soldier equivalent against danger × 2). A win brings back an item lying there, a book or an old treasure, or only the tale; a loss costs a third of the party and maybe the leader, whose items stay in the place for the next company. Robbing another people's barrow is a grievance and may wake the restless dead (a new great creature). Great beasts now hoard what they kill and what their raids carry off; a great beast's hide, horn or scales may become an artifact. 10 seeds at 256: 90 places, 25 barrows and 57 expeditions per world (12 treasures, 5 books, 12 empty-handed, 28 failed), 2.9 leaders lost, 6 commoners made heroes; population −5%, legends 7.9 to 10.6, the rest within noise; history 4.68 s per world against main's 4.86 s on the same machine. The map layer and a Places page come next. Fixtures re-pinned for history drift (named in each test); baseline regenerated. Mock-up: `/mnt/project-files/worldgen/M4.7/mockup.md`.
- **Dwarven mountain holds** (M4.10, quick win chosen by Morgan; "Holds + more births" approved on a card). A hold is a new kind of place: Dvergar capitals are holds (were fortresses) and a Dvergar town founded on high ground becomes one; a hold never grows into a town. Holds defend at x1.6 (a fortress x1.5). High ground feeds a hold x1.5 and a pass x1.2 on top of ore x3, since holds trade ore for food.

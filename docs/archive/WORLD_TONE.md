# Middle fantasy: approved starting tone

**Status:** baseline setting direction approved. These are narrative/system guidelines, not finished mechanics or numerical balance.

## Position between low and high fantasy

The world should feel grounded at its base: people need food, shelter, safety, materials, kinship, and institutions. But magic, unusual life, and supernatural phenomena really exist and occasionally transform ordinary social systems. Magic should neither be wholly hidden nor so universal that material constraints cease to matter.

| Domain | Initial middle-fantasy approach | Future room to expand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settlements | Agriculture, labor, construction, health, economy and leadership remain central | Magical cities, infrastructure and far larger civilizations |
| Magic | Real but unevenly distributed; bounded in cost, access, and effects | Widespread spell traditions, enchanted infrastructure |
| Creatures | Ordinary ecology with occasional fantastical animals, unusual species and dangerous beings | More varied ecologies, sapient species and magical migration |
| Culture | Institutions, traditions and beliefs respond to historical experiences | Mage orders, magical theocracies, otherworldly factions |
| Geography | Understandable biomes, resources and climate, with special locations | Supernatural regions and radically altered geographies |
| Historical stories | Discoveries ripple through food, migration, resources, status and politics | Arcane catastrophes, mythical eras, high-magic geopolitical upheavals |

**Design rule:** no fantasy event merely to add lore flavor. If a village discovers an arcane spring, it has real affordances, drawbacks and consequences for population, labor, religion, migration, trade, health or politics, and the chronicle can trace them.

## Illustrative world history (NOT guaranteed script)

A remote village learns that water from a rare spring heals wounds faster. More travelers arrive, raising demand for food and shelter. A family claims the spring, a religious group disputes the claim, an elected council imposes access rules, and merchants divert trade routes. A later drought might force choices about irrigation versus healing. Outcomes depend on economic conditions and relationships, not a fixed narrative sequence.

## Scoping

1. **Phase 1:** fully causal mundane settlement simulation; optional terrain labels should not imply already implemented magic.
2. **Phase 2:** people, relationships and histories create the foundations for magic-related social consequences later.
3. **Phase 3:** implement exactly one bounded supernatural mechanism as an example of magic with grounded consequences (subject to separate design review).
4. **Phase 4+:** let multiple settlements react differently to resources and magical phenomena; grow variety incrementally.
5. **Phase 6:** consider adding more fantasy systems only after testing what creates authentic divergence. Do not implement separate low/medium/high profiles as a Phase 1 requirement.

## Not yet decided

- Specific rules for magical energy, spells, creatures, intelligent species, religion, cosmology, or technology.
- Exact rarity and impact distributions.
- Whether different fantasy intensity settings will be packaged into one generator in a future version.

Do **not** settle these through unrelated implementation choices. Propose and approve them as their systems become relevant.
